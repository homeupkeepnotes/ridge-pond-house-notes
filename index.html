Will the Boat Fit? Garage Door Height and Clearance for Candlewood Lake Households in Brookfield

November 25, 2026

A good part of Brookfield's western edge runs along Candlewood Lake, and the lake shapes how people here use their garages. Between the coves off Candlewood Lake Road and the neighborhoods that climb the hills above the water, it is common to find a pontoon or a small runabout on a trailer, a pickup to tow it, kayaks on a rack, and a roof box on the family car for most of the summer. At the end of the season, a lot of that equipment needs a dry place to spend the winter.

That is when owners discover that the garage door was sized for a sedan. Before you try to back a trailer in, or before you plan a new or taller door, a few measurements will tell you what is actually possible.

The opening is only part of the story

Most residential garage doors are around seven feet tall, and many older houses have that height or less. A boat on a trailer, a lifted truck, or an SUV with a cargo box can easily be taller than that. But the door height is only the first limit.

When the door is open, it does not disappear. It rolls up and back along horizontal tracks, and it hangs below the ceiling. The bottom edge of an open door, the opener rail, the opener motor, and any light fixtures all hang lower than the ceiling itself. The real clearance is the lowest of those points, not the height of the opening.

Measure before you load up

With the door fully open, measure:

  • From the floor to the bottom edge of the open door at the front of the garage.
  • From the floor to the bottom of the opener rail and the motor unit.
  • The depth of the garage from the inside of the closed door to the back wall.
  • The width of the opening, and how much room the trailer tongue and the boat's beam leave on each side.

Then measure the tallest point of the boat on its trailer, including windshields, bimini frames, outboard motors tilted up, and anything strapped on top. Allow a margin; trailers bounce, and floors are not always level.

Ways to gain height inside

If the opening is tall enough but the open door or opener is in the way, the hardware can sometimes be rearranged.

  • High-lift tracks carry the door farther up the wall before it turns back, which raises the bottom edge of the open door. They need extra room above the opening.
  • Wall-mounted openers sit beside the door on the torsion bar instead of hanging from a rail in the middle of the ceiling, which frees up the center of the garage.
  • Moving lights and storage racks that hang in the path of a tall load is often the easiest fix of all.

Which option fits depends on ceiling height, the space above the header, and how the existing springs are set up.

When the opening itself needs to change

If the boat or truck is simply taller than the door, the only real answer is a taller opening. That is a framing project as well as a door project. The header may need to be raised, which can involve structural work on the wall above, and a taller door needs different springs and tracks. Garages built into the slopes around the lake sometimes have a room or a deck above them, which affects what can be done.

If you are considering it, plan the opening and the door together, and think about how the door will look on the house. A taller door on a single-story garage can change the proportions of the front of the home.

Lake moisture and winter storage

A garage that holds a boat over the winter also holds some of the lake. Boats and trailers come off the water damp, and covers trap moisture underneath. In a closed garage that can mean condensation on the door's inner face and extra moisture around the springs and cables. Let the boat dry before closing it up, and keep an eye on the bottom section and seal through the winter.

Moving a trailer near the door

Backing a trailer through a garage door takes practice, and the door tracks and the bottom section are the parts most likely to get hit. A spotter, a mark on the floor, and a slow approach save a lot of trouble. If a track does get bumped, do not run the door until it has been checked; a bent track can throw rollers and leave the door hanging.
